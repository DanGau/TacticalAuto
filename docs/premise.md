# Premise

**Genre.** Isometric superhero auto battler, against an alien invasion. The player owns a small team of heroes who fight on their own, kicking down the door, and builds each one up over a run.

**Story.** Aliens have landed at the South Pole; their hub lies under the Antarctic ice. The player gathers an elite squad of the world's last heroes, crossing the globe continent by continent to reach each one, then raids the hub. The aliens hunt the squad harder with every stop it makes. A brief story opens each run, before its first choice.

**Tone.** Science fantasy with a comic book's swagger: heroes, powers and alien artifacts are welcome; magic by that name is not.

**Loop.** Roguelike. A won run saves the world; then more aliens arrive and the next run begins.

**Battle.** A town somewhere in the world, 96 tiles square, many screens across. The camera frames what is in play and follows the action, a drag pans it, and it shows past the map only to keep the squad in frame. The fight is staged in an arena within it, about 30 tiles across: the aliens hang out there, objects stand between them in open ground, and the squad never wanders the map to find them. The player's decisions are which of three superhero entrances the squad makes, and when to use its consumables. Like a film, the game cuts straight to the entrance at the fight: kicking the door in, charging through a wall, landing and monologuing, crashing through a skylight, rappelling onto high ground, striding out of smoke, ramming a truck into them, or walking in behind an artillery strike, for threat. Each has a small perk; see `entrances.ts`. Nothing strikes the whole battlefield. Heroes never run: every fight is the squad against the aliens near it. The battle then plays out in alternating turns, humans first. In a turn every unit of the side moves at once, then each attacks, one after another: a soldier makes two attacks, its shot, swing or rocket twice, an alien one. A soldier may keep some of its move to walk to a shot between its attacks, and to step back after them. A soldier still uses at most one bigger ability a turn. Unit AI is predictable, so the player can foresee what an entrance will do.

**Encounters.** Each battle is designed as one set-piece, as a DM prepares an encounter (`encounter.ts`): a fight flows along an axis from the ways in to an overlook the aliens hold. The arena is open ground, buildings and clutter cleared; fuel tanks stand in the open; heaps of things to hurl lie by the flanks; a ledge rises by one way in. The three entrances each take their own approach: head on, by the ledge, by the heaps. Each alien gets a post of its own, as a DM places a miniature: shooters take the heights and the far side, bruisers the gaps between the heaps, two stand by what the mission is about (the uplink, the terror's heart, the VIP, the commander on the overlook), and the rest spread across the open. Reinforcements arrive at set gates behind and beside the aliens.

**Objects.** A hero uses up to three objects a battle in place of an attack: it hurls what stands beside it at an alien, or sets off a fuel tank beside the aliens. Heroes value a tile beside something to hurl.

**Engaging.** Each alien stands at its own post in the arena; none patrols. The entrance screen shows every alien on the map, but burrowers, which stay hidden underground. Every alien is alerted the moment the heroes burst in, and, as in a kung-fu film, they come at the heroes a few at a time: those nearest the entrance engage at once, and more join every two rounds or once none in the fight is left standing, up to twice the soldiers on the field and two more. An alien waiting its turn holds its post and its fire, and joins if hurt, or, room allowing, if a soldier comes within 5 tiles. Once the squad is in, fog darkens every tile no soldier sees. The squad never hides: every turn is a full turn, and soldiers march on the fight at their full move from the first. The squad moves as one: every soldier closes on the same enemy, the one nearest the squad's centre, and stays near that centre. A landing therefore decides which aliens the squad meets first and on what ground. Heroes never take cover or weigh what could shoot them: a soldier with a shot takes the tile with the best shot, counting height and something to hurl. A soldier with no shot dashes on to one if it can, else takes a standard action in place of attacking: with an alien able to reach it next turn it prepares, overwatching to shoot the first alien to come in range; otherwise it sprints, moving again toward the fight or the way out. While any alien is in sight, soldiers avoid stepping where they would draw in an alien waiting its turn, so one fight ends before the next begins. A soldier walled in punches through the walls about it. On the march, Deadeyes keep behind the rest of the squad.

**Power curve.** An alien kind's health never changes, so the aliens that are frightening to one soldier are mown down by four built-up ones. The aliens come in numbers: a fight going the squad's way looks the same, but one slipping turns deadly fast. Difficulty rises in ways the player can see: the alien force grows with each leg, new kinds appear, and deeper aliens are better equipped, with a point of armor for each step along the road and a point of damage for every third.

**Health and armor.** Every unit shows its health and armor as pips, one each, ten to a row: health in red, health lost in dark, armor in yellow. Armor is temporary health: it takes damage before health does, and is whole again at the start of each fight. It reduces no hit.

**Aliens.** Aliens win by rules no soldier has, after XCOM's aliens and StarCraft's Zerg. A mission has a force to spend on themed handfuls of aliens: four swarmlings, two spitters with a trooper, a psion and two troopers, and so on; deeper along the road, more appear. Each then stands alone. Deeper along the road come aliens that each punish a squad lacking one answer and fold to a squad that has it: a juggernaut's plates to many hits or shred, an incubator to burst before it hatches, a wraith to crits, burrowers that surface beside the loneliest soldier to overwatch, a feeder that grows on every trick to plain shooting, a regenerator to fire, sprinters to chill, a hive node to reach, and a mirror, which hits as hard as the squad's hardest hitter, to a squad whose strength is spread. An Emplacement is a turret, an automated gun that never moves and shoots any soldier in its sights, with a trooper beside it. The first time the player meets one, the battle pauses on a full screen of it, its name and one calm line on what it will do, until the player acknowledges it. The flight map tells only how many aliens of each danger, small, medium or large, a stop holds; a Recon Uplink relic names them. Before the entrance, the player is shown every alien where it stands, and what each kind does.

- Grunt: shoots from range, picking its ground.
- Swarmling: weak, fast, attacks only up close, and comes in numbers.
- Spitter: its spit leaves acid that burns whoever is still in it after their side moves.
- Burster: runs at the squad and explodes, beside a soldier or wherever it dies.
- Brute: tough, attacks only up close, and charges whoever hits it.
- Psion: panics a soldier, who loses a turn of action; its death hurts the aliens near it.
- Harvester: on rescues; fast, attacks only up close, and a civilian it kills rises next turn as a husk, a weak alien that attacks up close.
- Boss: guards an extraction or the hub. Each hero already extracted makes the next boss tougher and adds a trooper to its escort; from the second extraction on it spawns swarmlings.

**Attacks.** Every attack hits, and every attack that deals damage deals at least 1, so damage is steady and the squad wears down battle by battle. What changes it is height and skills; crits come at their stated chance. Tall objects and walls block shots; nothing shoots through a wall. An alien moves to where its best shot most outweighs the best shot coming back, bolder at full health and more careful as it is wounded. A badly wounded soldier fights from out of arm's reach while a healthier squadmate fights on.

**Maps.** Each battlefield is built from objects, after XCOM 2's plots and parcels: roads cut it into lots, and each lot is a place, such as a house with rooms and furniture, a park of trees, a car park, a fenced depot of crates, a warehouse, or an open field. Vehicles stand on the roads. A zone on the road names the kind of battlefield its battles are fought in: town, city, countryside, industrial yard, or ruins. Among their lots are squares with a monument, markets, orchards with lanes between the trees, graveyards, and buildings fallen in. A satellite stands in a walled compound with a gate on each side. The alien source is a hive of spires and growths with nothing human in it. Walls and windows stand on the lines between tiles, with doorways through them; a soldier shoots through a window and not through a wall. Trees, trucks and stacks are tall, blocking movement and shots; cars, crates, fences and windows are low, blocking movement alone. A rocket or grenade wrecks the objects and walls it catches, opening new lines of fire. Roofs within the arena's circle, and over any unit in view, are cut away, so what stands inside shows; the rest stay on. Post-apocalyptic cities and countryside are the setting.

**Run.** A run begins by picking the soldier who leads the squad, a small globe turning to the city they wait in. A run is three legs, each crossing a continent to a city where an elite hero holds out: the leg ends in an extraction, a guarded fight that adds the hero to the squad, seasoned. Then a last leg leads to the alien hub; winning that final mission saves the world. The map, Flight, is a globe filling the screen under the header, with no instructions, framed on where the squad stands and the stops ahead, far enough in that no two crowd, and turned so the way on leads up: each stop a step along the way across the continent, always at a real town, the steps uneven and the trails never crossing; the journey so far in gold, trails dotted from where the squad stands to each choice, the choices pulsing. One tap on a stop picks it and lights the way on from it; a second flies the squad's helicopter there, and into a fight dives down through the clouds. The globe glides from view to view, the choices fading in as it settles. A battle's town is built the way its continent builds.

**Road.** A leg is a map of trails that part and meet, nine stops long whichever way is taken, then its extraction. Each stop leads to one, two or three in the next column, and the player picks among them. It shows the stop the squad stands on and the stops still in reach up to three ahead; stops behind and stops beyond sight stay hidden. A leg's first stops are battles; the rest are drawn at random: a battle, a shop, an unknown stop, a medic, or a supply drop or gear cache, which need no fight. At a medic (✚) the squad rests and heals fully; a squad already at full health instead takes a piece of rare gear or a level for its lowest soldier. Each battle has its own risk, goal and kind of battlefield: a quiet one has fewer aliens and pays no gear; a contested one is a fair fight with an even chance of a piece of gear; an overrun one has more aliens and pays a piece of rare gear. So a choice weighs different journeys, not easy against hard. An extraction and the alien hub are each guarded by a boss.

**Missions.** Each battle has a goal, and each goal pays its own reward, so the road chosen steers the build. Clear them out: kill every alien, for supplies and gear. Hold and assassinate missions appear from the second leg, so a lone first soldier never meets them. Takedown: kill a commander and the rest flee, for rare gear; it is armored, comes with two guards, three deeper along the road, survives any one shot from full health, and blinks to safety once brought to half. Hold the uplink: the uplink stands at the arena's heart and the entrances by it; end six rounds with a soldier in its zone while a few aliens come in every second round, at a gate clear of the squad; then an extraction zone appears in the edge, and they keep coming until every soldier left has lifted off from it, for a relic; aliens take the uplink, then go for the squad. Rescue: a terror site. Ten civilians are scattered about the terror's heart, and the squad bursts in among them. The aliens come for the squad, but an alien with no good shot at a soldier shoots a civilian instead, a civilian's shot counting for less than a soldier's. So a squad that hangs back loses civilians, and one that pushes in, taking the risk, saves more. Civilians flee the aliens toward the squad, and the squad, never cautious here, moves as one on the alien nearest a civilian, or with none near one, the nearest civilian, a soldier taking a chance to reach a civilian over a shot; a soldier, or a mech it called down, beside a civilian gets them out. Saving more than half wins it; saving half or fewer loses it, and it pays nothing. Won, each saved pays supplies and every soldier standing experience, and every four left behind add a threat; it ends once none is left. VIP rescue, from the second leg: a VIP is held in a building within, two aliens standing guard. The first soldier within 3 tiles blows in the wall between them, catching the guards so they do not shoot on their next turn; a soldier within 3 tiles frees them once no alien in the fight that can move stands within 10 tiles. Then an extraction zone appears in the edge, a walk of about 24 tiles away. The VIP follows the squad there, keeping within 2 tiles of a soldier, while the squad keeps by the VIP; nothing hurts a held VIP, but the aliens shoot a freed one before anyone. The VIP lifts off at the zone, handed on by any soldier there, and that wins it; losing the VIP loses the mission. Won, it pays supplies and a relic. Smash and grab: guarded caches; each grabbed pays supplies, and each sought beyond the first adds a threat. Bring them back: never drawn; it comes when the aliens hold soldiers (see Death). Hovering a battle on the map counts its aliens by kind.

**Threat.** One bar for the whole invasion, and the run's main race. Every stop adds two, and a lost battle more, so a longer road costs more. The hunt bites as the bar fills: every battle has more aliens, up to half again at full, and the map's force for a stop counts them. Power is sold for threat: the Danger Close entrance's artillery strike and a raid's extra caches. When it is full, the squad is ambushed on the road before the next stop: survive the ambush, which empties the bar if won. Unpaid down, it fills about once a leg. As in War of the Chosen, the squad is hunted: there is no entrance and no planning; the game cuts straight to the squad in the arena, the Hunter engaged first, a mini-boss about half again as strong as the squad's strongest soldier. The squad stands and fights to the last alien, and almost always ends it bruised. Ambushes are meant to be the main way a run is lost.

**Start.** A title screen leads to the run's first choice, as Slay the Spire's opening blessing: three recruits of different classes, each card showing its class, ability, first skill and trait. One is plain; each other has a trait, a strength paid for with a weakness, such as joining at level 3 for threat, or more range for less health.

**Overworld.** Between stops the player moves among three views: the Map, which shows the whole run as trails of stops and the ways on; the Base, which lists the squad's keywords, doctrines and relics; and the Barracks, which shows each soldier, its gear, skills and feats. After a stop come the choices it left, one screen each: a relic, each piece of gear found, each skill owed. A skill screen names the soldier and its options, and nothing of what lies after. Then one debrief reveals the threat and supplies, and each soldier by class badge, name and weapon, its experience bar filling and flaring on a level gained, and feats it pulled off; beneath them a crate for each find. The player then returns to the Map.

**Losing.** A run is lost only in a battle, never on the map, and suddenly rather than slowly. Losing an ambush, an extraction, a recovery or the final mission ends the run, as does a wipe. Losing any other battle adds threat and earns nothing. Health carries from battle to battle, as in Slay the Spire: each stop travelled to heals every soldier 1, and a soldier downed comes back at 1. So a strong squad rampages, and one bad fight sends it the safe way, toward a medic. The Barracks, the debrief and the battle show each soldier's health and armor.

**Progression.**

- Shops: won battles, supply drops and sold gear pay supplies, spent only at shops on the road: five pieces of gear with an effect, each bought for the soldier the player picks; four relics; three consumables; and a bribe that lowers the threat. A squad can never buy one out. Each shop rolls its own stock, and no two pieces of gear in it share an effect. What is bought stays in its place, greyed out and marked Purchased, so the shop never shifts under the player's hand.
- Consumables: as Slay the Spire's potions, the squad carries up to three, each used once. Shops sell them, and a won battle now and then turns one up. In battle each shows in a slot beside the turn dial, and the player taps one to use it at the next beat; one acting on the squad can be readied on the entrance screen, to take effect as it goes in, and one acting on the aliens waits until an alien is in the fight. Among them: an airstrike on the aliens nearest the squad, which adds threat; a medkit; stims and rage serum, each strength paid for with health; armor plating; a flashbang, smoke, a cryo bomb, a firebomb and a target painter on every alien in the fight; a sentry turret; and a decoy that draws the aliens' fire.
- Unknown stops: as Slay the Spire's question marks, revealed on arrival as a battle, a shop, or an event met once a run. An event offers options that each help and hurt. Some adopt a doctrine that changes the rules for the rest of the run: Mass Cloning (two more clones, less health), Lone Wolf (one soldier, much stronger, no recruits), Specialists (every soldier a different class), Exosuits (armor, for move), Fortification (every hit on either side does less), Scatterguns (damage for range). Weapon doctrines change how the whole squad fights, and the squad fights one way at a time: Blademasters (melee only, charging in, blunting shots from range), Psi Operatives (attacks through walls, ignoring armor), Demolitions (each hit wrecks the objects around its target), Gunslingers (a third shot a turn). Others trade supplies, threat, soldiers and relics.
- Squad: the run begins with one soldier. Each extraction won adds a hero, to four for the final mission; a hero joins at level 2, its skills learned, with a trait of its own, met on a screen of its own before the debrief. In the debrief each soldier owed a skill has a button to choose it, and each find is a crate to open; both open in a pop-up over the debrief, which stays still behind, and the crate asks which soldier takes the find, showing what each would replace and how its stats would change, or sells it. Every soldier who fought gains experience, downed or not: a little shared for fighting and winning, the rest earned by what each did, its kills and damage done, so a soldier who carries a fight snowballs. A feat, such as killing an alien at full health with one hit, pays a little more the first time a soldier pulls it off, so later recruits can catch up by doing what the veteran already has. Every soldier has one of eight classes, drawn at random: the Vanguard, Powerhouse, Deadeye and Beacon; the Gadgeteer, who deploys a combat mech once aliens are in sight, or a swarm of light drones with Drone Swarm; the Pyro, whose napalm sets a knot of aliens and their ground alight; the Cryo, whose every hit chills; and the Phantom, a superhero who cloaks when aliens have it in their sights: cloaked, no alien sees or targets it, and its next attack strikes out of nowhere, harder and past armor, and reveals it. The cloak is a power, not sneaking: the Phantom fights every turn like any hero, and shimmers, faint, on the player's screen. The Pyro and Cryo start a burn or frost build. A mech is a frail decoy, 3 health, that every alien able to shoot it must shoot; it fights for the squad, and its kills are its engineer's, but it cannot hold the field alone. Drones, 2 health each, are free for the aliens to ignore. A class is a set of stat changes; a way of positioning, such as a Deadeye hanging back, which its weapon may override; and one ability the soldier uses unprompted. An ability either replaces the soldier's shot, as a Powerhouse's rocket does, or comes on top of it, as a Beacon's heal does.
- Levels and skills: levels never stop; each takes more experience than the last. Each class has a tree of a dozen or more skills, each a guard skill that keeps the squad alive (shrugging off damage, absorbing a hit, armor, decoys, healing), a control skill that holds or moves the aliens (roots, stuns, knockbacks, throws, walls of fire), or a power skill that hits harder; every tree has roots of all three. A recruit arrives knowing a root drawn at random; at each of its next three levels it is offered three skills its tree has opened to it, one of each kind, drawn for that soldier, and learns one; each skill opens those that follow it, and one passed over may come again. Later levels add only stats. So two soldiers of a class differ from run to run. Many skills are a hero's: a Vanguard leaps into a knot of aliens and throws them, a Powerhouse slams the ground or roars them into a stupor, a Gadgeteer pulses an EMP, a Beacon blinds them with a flashbang. Skills borrow from tabletop role-playing: a reaction shot on the enemy's turn, bonus damage on an exposed target, auras that help squadmates nearby, marking a target, getting a fallen squadmate up, a second action once a battle, forcing the enemy to target the tank. Whenever a skill, a piece of gear or a relic shapes a shot or acts on a unit, its name shows over that unit, so the player sees each one at work.
- Relics: winning an extraction, a hold, a VIP rescue, an ambush or an Overrun battle offers three relics, and the squad keeps one for the run. A relic changes every soldier or how the run pays, as Slay the Spire's do, so each run is built around what it found. Many play on a mechanic: entrance relics offer a fourth entrance or always the monologue, blast, strip or chill the aliens where the squad comes in, raise barricades or bring a turret, so they make the entrance matter more; Shredder and Cryo Charges are offered only once the squad holds Breaching Charges. Others serve rescues, holds, ambushes and bosses.
- Keywords: Burn, Frost, Mark, Crit, Kill and Ward tie skills, gear and relics together, as a deckbuilder's card types do. Some pieces cause a keyword, such as gear that chills or a skill that marks; others pay off on it, such as a relic that adds damage to chilled targets. Two keywords can meet: a crit that sets a target burning, a hit on a target both burning and chilled. Cards colour each keyword in their text, lit where the squad already has one, and define it in a box on hover, and the first relic offered builds on one the squad has.
- Resists: some aliens shrug off a keyword, so no build is safe everywhere. Bursters cannot burn, brutes cannot be chilled, psions cannot be marked, and spitters' acid ignores armor. Each leg's boss resists one keyword, shown from the start of the leg so the squad can build to beat it. 
- One entrance per mission, three kinds drawn at random, each placed where its kind fits, by the fight: an assassination's never near the commander, so the heroes fight through its minions first; a hold's by the uplink; a VIP rescue's by the VIP's building; a rescue's among the civilians; a raid makes one entrance and chooses how many of its guarded caches to go for; an ambush none at all. The monologue spares civilians and a VIP; which other entrance suits depends on the squad; the game does not say.
- Map layouts: each battlefield's plot lies over a shape that decides where the fighting funnels: an open grid of streets; a river crossed only at bridges; a coast that walls one side with water; a blast crater, open and coverless in the middle; or long walls that cut three lanes joined at a few gaps. Water blocks standing but not shots.
- Mission AI: the squad fights to its goal. On a hold it heads for the uplink and stands its ground within 3 tiles of it, and the aliens converge there; on an assassination it closes on the commander once sighted; on a rescue it pushes in, careless of risk, on the alien nearest a civilian; on a VIP rescue it makes for the VIP, then escorts it out. Each battle's alien force varies by one either way.
- High ground: hills rise a level, their crests two. A unit sees 2 tiles farther for each level it stands above what it looks at, and 2 less for each below; a shot does 1 more damage per level downhill and 1 less uphill; and a step up a level costs 2 extra move, so charging a hill is slow. Raised tiles are drawn higher and lighter.
- Gear: gear is rare. A won battle drops at most one piece, by its goal and risk; a won extraction and a cache drop one. It goes to the squad, not to any one soldier. Each is built from parts, as Borderlands builds guns: a base, a stat mod, some of which trade one stat for another, or on rare and epic gear one effect, such as setting targets burning or absorbing the first hit. A soldier has three slots: weapon, armor, utility. Found gear is decided at once: the player puts it on any soldier, replacing and selling what that soldier had in the slot, or sells it. Nothing is taken off or stored. Gear survives a death.
- Weapons: a weapon's base is the weapon itself, and any class wields any weapon. Every soldier starts with an assault rifle; finds bring sniper rifles that hang back and shoot from afar, shotguns that close in, SMGs that fire a third time a turn, machine guns that suppress, and melee weapons that reach only the next tile and charge in: a hammer that knocks aliens back, a sword that cleaves, a shield that adds armor and blunts shots from range, and power fists that strike again on a kill. A weapon's way of positioning, where it has one, overrides its wielder's class's.
- Death: nobody is left behind, and nobody is replaced. A soldier brought to nothing is downed and lies where it fell. A squad that wins with a soldier standing on the field carries every soldier home. A wipe, every soldier in a battle downed and none lifted off, is game over, even if the last alien fell too. Getting out of a hold, a soldier with its hands free goes for the nearest squadmate down, picks them up and carries them out, walking at half its move; a carrier who falls drops them. Soldiers left downed are taken, and the next stop is a recovery, before the map goes on: the aliens hold them beside their posts, at a third of the leg's force, while all of them come again, a few every second round. A soldier who reaches a captive gets them up at half health to fight. The recovery is all or nothing: win, and every captive rejoins; lose, and the run ends.
